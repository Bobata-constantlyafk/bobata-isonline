  - title: The Truman Show
    meta: 1998 · PETER WEIR
    imageUrl: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/cd/Trumanshow.jpg/500px-Trumanshow.jpg
    review: |-
      A man discovers his entire life has been rigged for an audience — the fake weather, the actors playing neighbors, the sun that clocks in on schedule. Weir plays it as comedy for the first act and dread for the rest, which is the correct order.

      What holds up isn't the twist. It's Truman's argument with Christof at the end — the creator defending the cage as kindness. Every algorithm since has made the same pitch.

      > "We accept the reality of the world with which we're presented."

      Watch it again after a year of feeds curated for you specifically. It stops being science fiction.
  - title: Pulp Fiction
    meta: 1994 · QUENTIN TARANTINO
    imageUrl: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/3/3b/Pulp_Fiction_%281994%29_poster.jpg/500px-Pulp_Fiction_%281994%29_poster.jpg
    review: |-
      Nonlinear before nonlinear was a film-school checkbox. Tarantino cuts the timeline into pieces and trusts you to reassemble it, which is still rarer than it should be thirty years on.

      The dialogue does the actual work — the Royale with Cheese bit isn't a joke, it's characterization, and it's doing it before either man has fired a shot.

      > "Say 'what' again. I dare you, I double dare you."

      Redemption arrives sideways, through a diner robbery and a briefcase nobody explains. Still the best use of restraint in Tarantino's whole filmography.

  - title: Ferris Bueller's Day Off
    meta: 1986 · JOHN HUGHES
    imageUrl: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9b/Ferris_Bueller%27s_Day_Off.jpg/500px-Ferris_Bueller%27s_Day_Off.jpg
    review: |-
      The rare teen movie that isn't actually about being a teenager — it's about the exact moment you realize adulthood is coming for you whether you're ready or not, and deciding to skip school anyway.

      Cameron is the real main character. Ferris just gets the title card. Everything Ferris does is a performance for Cameron's benefit, which the film never quite says out loud but never hides either.

      > "Life moves pretty fast. If you don't stop and look around once in a while, you could miss it."

      Corny on paper. Earned by the time the credits roll.
